# V41 charcoal practice study

Commissioned for V41; sources inspected 10 October 2026. This is a practice-to-operation study, not a claim that our renderer reproduces real charcoal physics. Artist instructions, manufacturer demonstrations and museum technical findings agree on an important sequence: control a broad tone, develop selected dark marks, and construct lights by removal as well as by leaving paper untouched.

## Material distinction

Matt Fussell distinguishes easily erased, lighter vine charcoal from darker, gum-bound compressed charcoal, which is harder to erase. He describes paper tooth and paper white contributing to visible values. These are material differences, not merely different shades of grey. [The Virtual Instructor, Charcoal Drawing Techniques](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/charcoal.html).

Charcoal is crumbly, dusty and matte; graphite has a more ordered structure and smoother, comparatively reflective marks. Jackson's explains that pressure changes charcoal's visible density and that its particles catch paper tooth; compressed stick sides can make broad marks. A grained grey oil stroke would omit these deposition and removal behaviours. [Jackson's, A Guide to Drawing](https://www.jacksonsart.com/en-us/a-guide-to-drawing).

V41 should be described as a **single stylised dry charcoal material**. It can borrow the controllability of a stick/pencil and the liftability of loose charcoal without claiming an exact named product. Its fixed darkest tone, deposition law and removal law are simulation choices. It does not model binders, hardness grades, fixation, paper abrasion or free dust.

## Practice translated into deterministic operations

The middle column states sourced practice. The right column proposes our approximation; equations, bounds and candidate schedules are not supplied by these sources.

| Technique | Sourced artistic behaviour | Practical simulation and study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad side shading | A stick side covers broad regions; lighter pressure makes lighter values. [Jackson's guide](https://www.jacksonsart.com/en-us/a-guide-to-drawing) | A broad footprint deposits a bounded amount of charcoal. Fit its strength to current material and desired tone. Demonstrate light/middle/dark side passes before judging full drawings. Broad should not mean an unavoidable dark rectangle. |
| Tip and edge | Derwent's Debbie Adams recommends a compressed-stick edge for fine detail, rotating corners to maintain a point. [Compressed charcoal tips](https://www.derwentart.com/en-gb/a/423935/compressed-charcoal:-ten-top-tips!) | Reuse the deposition operation with a narrower footprint; distinguish edge/tip contact in records or study labels. Bound length and width by local form. Do not create detail by long narrow strokes that cross silhouettes. |
| Pressure and layering | Pressure affects darkening; material type affects maximum darkness and erasability. [Jackson's guide](https://www.jacksonsart.com/en-us/a-guide-to-drawing), [Fussell](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/charcoal.html) | Maintain deposited amount separately from paper. Repeated low-strength passes should deepen a tone predictably toward a fixed dark limit. Test a value ramp and equal repeated passes; do not clamp every useful light deposit into the same dense slab. |
| Paper tooth | Tooth catches dry particles; smooth and rough papers give different results. [Fussell](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/charcoal.html), [Jackson's guide](https://www.jacksonsart.com/en-us/a-guide-to-drawing) | Use one anchored paper field. Pressure-dependent pickup uses that same field on every pass. Grain should modulate tone locally, not introduce a fresh noise pattern per stroke or dominate the form. Lift reveals the original field. |
| Hatching and directional shading | Adams describes crosshatching followed by stumping for a misty effect. [Derwent compressed charcoal tips](https://www.derwentart.com/en-gb/a/423935/compressed-charcoal:-ten-top-tips!) | Show parallel narrow deposits and a crossing second layer without blending first. Line direction and spacing establish tone while preserving paper gaps. Planner directions may follow reference structure, but the studies must disclose whether hatch families are actually proposed by the planner. |
| Hard, soft and broken edges | Adams softens sharp edges with a paper stump; tooth and light contact can leave broken marks. [Derwent compressed charcoal tips](https://www.derwentart.com/en-gb/a/423935/compressed-charcoal:-ten-top-tips!), [Jackson's guide](https://www.jacksonsart.com/en-us/a-guide-to-drawing) | Demonstrate bounded hard edges, tapered contact and grain-broken low pressure. A feathered deposit is a useful stylised soft contact edge; it is **not** a stump operation, which redistributes existing material. Report that distinction. |
| Broad lift and precise highlights | Kneadable erasers remove charcoal; a rubber shaper or plastic eraser edge makes a thin light line, and an eraser shield confines sharp-edged removal. [Derwent, David Bellamy tips](https://www.derwentart.com/a/187085) | The same footprint model may carry `deposit` or `lift`. Broad gentle and narrow precise lifting reduce the charcoal actually present; neither paints white nor rewinds history. Test light, layered and dense deposits and deposit → lift → redraw. |
| Reductive construction | A toned charcoal ground can be carved into highlights; putty erasers can be moulded to the required shape. Hard erasers can remove additional residue. [Louise Reynolds, Erasing Techniques for Dry Media](https://www.jacksonsart.com/blog/2026/02/06/erasing-techniques-for-dry-media/) | Use broad tone then selective lift to construct a light shape against dark ground. Permit candidates to lift only where a scored outcome improves the drawing; do not impose an erase quota. Untouched, partly deposited and lifted regions must remain distinguishable through operation history. |

## Museum evidence: construction, not a universal recipe

The Van Gogh Museum's technical examination of Redon's *Autumn Leaves* describes large horizontal powder-charcoal expanses producing a grey base, stick-tip details, then selective dough/gum removal of bright zones. This is direct evidence for a broad-tone/detail/lift construction, including landscape highlights. Redon's fixative and finger manipulations are outside V41. [Fleur Roos Rosa de Carvalho, Early Landscape Drawings in Charcoal (2022)](https://catalogues.vangoghmuseum.com/contemporaries-of-van-gogh-2/cat01-04).

Getty's Noir project identifies stumping and lifting in Bonheur's *Charcoal burners* and cautions that visually similar black media can differ materially. [Getty, Noir](https://www.getty.edu/projects/noir/). Getty researchers identified six black materials in Redon's *Apparition*, reconstructing broad blocking followed by other media for detail and darker passages. That evidence cannot justify labelling every velvety or sharp black mark as charcoal alone. [Daly et al., Heritage Science 7, 43 (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-019-0286-6).

## Is blending necessary now?

Blending is established practice: a stump rubs/spreads existing charcoal into softer adjacent tones. Bellamy recommends it; Fussell uses it in cast shadows. [Derwent pencil tips](https://www.derwentart.com/a/187085), [Fussell](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/charcoal.html). Adams also describes powdered charcoal on cloth for smooth broad coverage. [Derwent compressed tips](https://www.derwentart.com/en-gb/a/423935/compressed-charcoal:-ten-top-tips!).

**Recommendation: defer a blending operation for this baseline.** These sources demonstrate its utility, not that every convincing dry-stick drawing requires it. First determine whether fitted broad deposits, layered tones, appropriately bounded narrow marks and selective lifting construct the sphere/cylinder and evaluation forms. If those work but soft tonal transitions remain the dominant fault, redistribution becomes a supported next step. Do not use blur or final-image filtering as a substitute for drawing operations.

## Removal limits and decision order

Jackson's charcoal demonstration and eraser comparisons show that full recovery is not guaranteed; putty lifts dusty layers, and hard erasers can remove more. [Erasing Techniques](https://www.jacksonsart.com/blog/2026/02/06/erasing-techniques-for-dry-media/). For V41, a bounded fraction of deposited charcoal per lift is an honest simple approximation. If repeated lifts asymptotically approach clean paper, say so: this omits staining, eraser contamination, damage and compacted deposits. If exact full recovery is allowed, document it as a convenience, not sourced physical behaviour.

Priorities follow the demonstrated V40 failure: **first fit useful light/middle deposition; then permit corrective/reductive lifting; then size and orient accents to the form.** More grain, random candidates, compulsory hatching or compulsory blending do not address overshoot. Judge the value ladder, sphere/cylinder and negative-space study before claiming proficiency on recognisable subjects. The sources inform capabilities; evidence from canonical studies and matched painting runs must establish whether our implementation can use them.
